# SkillEX Proposal Deck Master Prompt

Use this prompt to generate a complete, copy-ready proposal presentation for SkillEX. The deck should work in Canva, PowerPoint, Gamma, NotebookLM, or any similar presentation tool.

Create a premium startup proposal presentation for SkillEX, a peer-to-peer skill exchange platform. The deck must feel like a serious investor, partner, and stakeholder pitch: visually premium, strategically credible, and grounded in the actual product. It should show what is already built, what is being improved, what is planned next, and how the business model sustains the platform without compromising access.

## Core Narrative

SkillEX helps people trade skills instead of money. A user can teach what they know, learn what they need, and build trust through real exchanges, reviews, community participation, and reputation. The platform should feel human, practical, and scalable.

The presentation must communicate four things clearly:

1. SkillEX is already a real product, not just an idea.
2. The roadmap is structured and honest about status.
3. The business model is sustainable and mission-aligned.
4. The credit economy makes the platform meaningfully different from other learning products.

## Global Visual Direction

- Tone: editorial, premium, modern, human, and founder-led
- Mood: startup proposal with investor-grade clarity
- Layout: asymmetric, magazine-like, with intentional whitespace
- Typography: bold headlines, restrained supporting text, clean hierarchy
- Imagery: real human photography and real product evidence where possible
- Composition: avoid generic templates, avoid busy infographic layouts, avoid decorative clutter
- Motion, if supported: minimal and elegant, never distracting

## Content Rules

- Clearly label feature status where relevant: Shipped Now, In Progress, Planned Next
- Never overstate unfinished work
- Make the business model credible and mission-protective
- Keep each slide visually simple, but conceptually strong
- Avoid tables, heavy cards, and crowded bullet blocks unless a layout absolutely needs them
- Use concise speaker notes for presenter delivery
- Make the deck persuasive to both technical and non-technical audiences

## Status Color System

- Shipped Now: solid cyan or teal
- In Progress: cyan outline or muted cyan
- Planned Next: light gray or soft neutral

## Slide-by-Slide Structure

### Slide 1 — Title

Goal: establish the brand and thesis immediately.

Layout:
- Full-bleed or split-screen with premium human photography
- One massive headline
- One short supporting line
- Minimal decorative treatment

Suggested headline:
Trade Knowledge, Not Cash.

Supporting line:
SkillEX is where learning, teaching, and trust happen in one place.

### Slide 2 — Team Intro

Goal: introduce the people behind the project with confidence and simplicity.

Use a clean, minimal layout with the team name and member list. Keep the slide more formal than decorative.

Title line:
Team: Deadliners

Member list:
- 0112230863 Md.Tanzamul Azad
- 011221580 Israt Jerin Porshi
- 0112231034 Tohid Bhuiyan

Optional supporting line:
The team building SkillEX combines product thinking, technical execution, and a shared vision for accessible peer learning.

### Slide 3 — The Problem

Goal: explain the need in plain language.

Show the real problem clearly:
- coaching and learning are expensive
- mentorship is often inaccessible
- many people have useful skills but no platform to share them
- traditional learning platforms create viewers more than participants

This slide should feel emotionally true, not academic.

### Slide 4 — The Solution

Goal: explain the product in one simple loop.

Use a visual flow:
Teach -> Match -> Exchange -> Review -> Grow

Supporting message:
Your knowledge becomes the currency.

### Slide 5 — What Already Works

Goal: show real product maturity and reduce skepticism.

Include the working experience already supported by the platform:
- registration and login
- onboarding and profile setup
- teach and learn skill preferences
- skill browsing and partner discovery
- smart matching
- exchange requests
- session lifecycle and completion
- reviews and ratings
- messaging and notifications
- community participation

This slide should feel like proof, not aspiration.

### Slide 6 — Feature Status Map

Goal: make the roadmap honest and visually clear.

Use a vertical stack or three-column structure with status headings:
- Shipped Now
- In Progress
- Planned Next

Content:

Shipped Now:
- user accounts and authentication
- profiles with teach and learn skills
- matching and exchange requests
- session flow and reviews
- messaging and community activity

In Progress:
- onboarding optimization
- trust and safety refinements
- analytics and KPI tracking
- notification improvements
- match quality tuning

Planned Next:
- university and institution dashboards
- advanced verification flows
- premium visibility tools
- stronger moderation tooling
- richer discovery and retention features

### Slide 7 — AI Matching Advantage

Goal: show why the platform is smarter than a directory.

Explain that SkillEX uses intelligent matching to connect people by skill compatibility, intent, and reputation.

Suggested framing:
- semantic similarity
- complementary skills
- reputation scoring
- intent matching

Keep the language accessible, confident, and practical.

### Slide 8 — Community Layer

Goal: show SkillEX as a living ecosystem, not just a marketplace.

Include the community features:
- community feed
- events
- discussions
- skill circles
- reputation over time

Message:
The community layer creates retention, trust, and repeat participation.

### Slide 9 — Business Model

Goal: explain how the platform makes money without charging for the core exchange.

Headline:
SkillEX never takes a cut from knowledge.

Supporting line:
The exchange stays free. Revenue comes from the infrastructure around it, not from the exchange itself.

Layout direction:
- Clean white slide
- Left side: large editorial headline and short thesis text
- Right side: simple vertical revenue stack, typography only, no cards, no table
- Top-right: a small candid photo of someone teaching in a coffee shop

Revenue stack format:
[STREAM NAME] .................. [STATUS LABEL]
One short description underneath

Use the following revenue streams:

01 SkillEX Pro .................. NOW
Verified badges, priority matching, analytics, and profile boosts. 200 to 300 BDT per month.

02 University Partnerships ..... NOW
Institutional subscriptions for universities and learning organizations. Bulk semester deals.

03 Skill Assessment + Badges ... 6 MONTHS
AI-generated tests, peer-reviewed tasks, and shareable certificates. 50 to 100 BDT per attempt.

04 Featured Events + Ticketing .. 6 MONTHS
Paid workshop ticketing with local payment support. Small platform fee from each transaction.

05 Skill Market + Credit Economy 1 YEAR
Teach others, earn Skill Credits, and use them to unlock premium skill packages.

06 B2B Team Workspaces ......... 1 YEAR
Private organization mode for startups and companies. Per-seat monthly subscriptions.

Closing line in coral italic:
A student in Dhaka with no budget can still access world-class peer learning. That is the mission. The business model protects it.

### Slide 10 — Skill Credit Economy

Goal: present the most distinctive mechanic in the deck.

This should be a dark slide for contrast and emphasis.

Headline:
Teach more. Earn more. Learn for free.

Explain the mechanic as a simple flow:
- teach a session -> earn credits
- earn more credits by teaching more
- spend credits on premium learning packages
- cash is optional, not mandatory

Core idea:
Knowledge itself becomes the payment system.

Add one clear line that casual users can still pay cash, but active contributors can earn access through teaching.

### Slide 11 — Go-To-Market

Goal: explain who will adopt first and why.

Target groups:
- students
- early-career professionals
- creators and freelancers
- university communities
- local learning groups
- startup teams

Explain why these groups are the strongest early adopters.

### Slide 12 — Roadmap

Goal: show how the product evolves over time.

Structure the roadmap as near-term, mid-term, and long-term.

Near-term:
- onboarding refinement
- match quality improvement
- trust and safety polish
- better activation and retention

Mid-term:
- institution partnerships
- analytics and reporting
- premium product expansion
- event and badge monetization

Long-term:
- credit economy
- B2B workspaces
- deeper ecosystem growth
- broader regional scale

### Slide 13 — Risk and Trust

Goal: show maturity and realism.

Acknowledge the real risks:
- moderation
- quality control
- user onboarding clarity
- trust and safety
- retention

Then explain that the platform reduces those risks through identity, reputation, feedback, and institutional partnerships.

### Slide 14 — Closing CTA

Goal: end with a strong human and business call to action.

Use a warm, hopeful photo and minimal text.

Suggested closing line:
What will you learn tomorrow?

Supporting line:
Someone in this room probably teaches exactly that.

Final message:
SkillEX connects the people who have the answers with the people who need them.

## Speaker Note Style

Every slide should include a short speaker note that sounds like a founder explaining the product to investors, partners, or a live audience. The tone should be clear, confident, and honest.

## Final Instruction

Make the presentation feel like a real proposal for a serious product with a meaningful mission, a working foundation, a realistic roadmap, and a scalable business model. It should be visually premium, strategically credible, and easy to present in a room full of investors, partners, or stakeholders.
